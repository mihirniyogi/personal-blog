Yesterday was my first...PILATES CLASS 🙌.

As soon as we stepped into [Kaya Health Club](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Z2fUaHKUyc7wVufW8), myself and a friend were greeted by this lush-smelling, posh-looking exercise club. It had state-of-the-art gyms, reformer equipment, showers etc. To get this for $5 (we signed up via our student accommodation) was a miracle.

We were a little anxious - it was our first class, and these workout sessions are known to be more popular amongst the ladies. That however, did not mean it was easy by any means. This has got to be one of the hardest, slow-burn workouts I've ever done in my life. Everything still hurts as I write this 😂 

### essence of pilates

From what I gather, the point of using the Reformer machine (and these exercises in general) is to have access to slow, controlled movements such that it really targets certain muscle groups. My legs were constantly shaking, but what made me feel better is seeing everyone else's legs shaking as well.  

### the machine

I'm VERY intrigued by the machine. Because of the weights, springs and slider mechanism, your body can transform into positions you would have never thought it could. 

### chit-chat

The instructor was well-versed with the movements, articulate, compassionate and encouraging. I approached her afterwards, just to have a little chat. After some small talk about how she's swam in the MBS infinity pool (wow everyone really likes this pool), she gave us a mini history dive into how pilates was created. Apparently it has its roots in physiotherapy for soldiers in the army; this machine was created by Joseph Pilates to help those with limb injuries.

### thoughts

I'm pretty intrigued by this class and am lowkey tempted to keep going when I return to Singapore. What I want to explore however are the benefits of the reformer pilates workout. Are there measurable goals? Do your muscles get toned? Maybe that will give me some motivation to continue going. Time to do a little research!
